What needs a look first

Two of the 30 authorities came back flagged, the costlier first. Each card names the reason, the passage and the paragraph it belongs to, and the correction, and prints the page it was read at.

Everything that cleared

26 authorities cleared: the authority exists as cited and the passage carries the point. Where one public search turned up a later decision, an amendment or a different standing in another jurisdiction, the row says so and still stands for the point it is cited for. That is not a citator noting-up, and the page says so directly under the counts. A cleared row still carries a remark where one helps: how Anns v. Merton stands in England and in Canada, the two amendments to Rule 42 since the filing, the judgment appealed from and what happened to it after this factum went in. One cleared row carries the remark a clerk would write in the margin: paragraph 95 of the factum puts the Occupiers’ Liability Act in the factum’s own words, and paragraph 105 sets out the statutory text it rests on, so the row is a remark, not a flag.

What could not be verified

Two authorities have no public copy: a 1996 Quicklaw citation and a passage from a 1972 treatise. Each row says what was tried and where to look, instead of a guess.

How it was checked

The same eight steps, in the order they ran. Every source with a public page was opened at that page and the address is on the row, so anyone with a browser can open the same page and check the row. Where a site refuses a plain read, a person opened the page in a browser and the row says so.

  1. 01

    The list

    Every authority in the table of authorities, and every citation in the text the table missed, was listed with the paragraphs that cite it, read off the document's own table, footnotes and quotations before any source was opened.

  2. 02

    The source, opened

    The system opened each authority one page at a time, the way a clerk would: the court's own decisions site first, because it is the source of record; then the legislature's consolidation; then CanLII, the public database where a clerk would also start. Where a site refused a plain read, a person opened the page in a browser and the row says who read it and when. The page read and the date are printed on every row.

  3. 03

    The citation, matched

    The case name, court, year and citation on the page were compared with the document's citation, and the style of cause was copied from the court's own page. A mismatch that changes which decision is meant is flagged with the corrected form; a difference in citation form the document copied from the court's own reasons is a remark. A citation that could not be found anywhere public is marked unverified, never guessed, and the row says where the authority lives.

  4. 04

    The passage, read

    The paragraphs citing the authority were read, the pinpoint the document gives was found on the source page, and the proposition was tested against the words there. Where the words carry it only with a qualification the document drops, that is reported as overstated and both texts are quoted, unless the document sets out the qualifying text elsewhere, in which case the row carries a remark naming both paragraphs. The argument itself is never graded.

  5. 05

    Quotations, word for word

    Every passage in quotation marks was compared with the source text at the place the footnote sends the reader, where the system read the source. A difference that changes meaning, or a quotation sent to the wrong case or paragraph, is flagged and both versions are shown. A row read from a person's report says not compared at source.

  6. 06

    History, on three levels

    First, the appellate history of every decision below the final court, on the court's site or the public database's history panel. Second, one search per authority for a later decision that overrules or restates the point, and for an authority from another jurisdiction one line on how it stands there. Third, the noting-up on a citator, on the firm's own subscription, which is the lawyer's.

  7. 07

    The results

    Cleared means the authority exists as cited and the passage carries the point; the citator noting-up has not been run. A later decision that displaces the point the document relies on is flagged as changed since the filing; a later decision, an amendment or a different standing elsewhere that leaves the point standing is a remark on a cleared row. Flagged names one of six reasons, printed on the card: not found, does not carry the point, overstated, misquoted, changed since the filing, citation error. Unverified means no public copy was reached and the row says what was tried and where the authority lives. The judgment appealed from is reported in the header, never as a flag.

  8. 08

    What was not done

    No opinion on the argument, no change to the document, no subscription service. The sources were read as they stood on the date above; a change after the filing is reported as a change, not as an error of the author.

What it does not do

  • No opinion on the argument. It checks that authorities exist, say what they are cited for, and what has happened to them since.
  • No change to the document. A citation error is corrected in the note; the lawyer makes the edit.
  • No citator noting-up. CanLII’s free noting-up lists the later decisions that cite an authority; the treatment signals on KeyCite Canada or QuickCITE need the firm’s own subscription, and the page names that pass as not done.
  • No search for what is missing. Authority the factum should have cited and did not is outside the check.
  • No check of the record. The other half of the same job, that each reference to the record is in the record, is outside this check.

Two honest limits:

  • A citation that lives only on a subscription service stays unverified, with what was tried on its row.
  • The CanLII and BAILII rows were read in a person’s browser, one page at a time, and each of those rows says so.

Nothing here touched a client file: the sample is a public filing, read as it stands on the court’s site. Review-ready, never send-ready: a lawyer approves everything before it leaves the firm. Night Docket is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Thomson Reuters or LexisNexis.

07 / FAQ

The questions, answered

How long does a check take?

About 90 minutes of the employee's own reading and writing, across 4 sittings, for a 42-page factum with 30 authorities; a person's time is not counted. 10 of the source pages had to be opened by a person in a browser, on 2026-09-11 and 2026-09-12, because CanLII and BAILII refuse a plain read. Every row says who read it and when, or that no public copy was reached.

What happens to the document?

The document stays in the firm's workspace, unchanged; the page goes back to the lawyer who sent it, PDF attached. Edward reads it under written business terms, and those terms go to the firm in writing before any real file is touched. On this sample no page a person opened in a browser was the document: all 10 were published case-law pages that refuse a machine read.

What does it not check?

The check reads what is cited, not what is missing, and not the record. The citator noting-up stays on the firm's own subscription; every other exclusion is listed under What it does not do.

What does unverified mean on the page?

Unverified means no public copy of the authority was reached. The row says what was tried and where the authority lives: the subscription service its citation belongs to, the reporter, or the tab of the book of authorities where the filing put a copy. It is a gap named plainly, not a finding that the authority is wrong.

Does the check change anything in the factum?

No. The document is read only. A flagged row gives the corrected citation or the words that differ in its note, in the form a clerk would write it, and the lawyer makes the change. Review-ready, never send-ready: a lawyer approves everything before it leaves the firm.

Claude Enterprise for law firms

By Jordy Backes Published 2026-08-10 Facts checked 2026-07-30

Answer

Claude Enterprise is Anthropic's top business plan: as listed July 30, 2026, $20 per seat per month plus usage billed at API rates, billed annually, adding admin and security controls (user provisioning, audit logs, custom data retention, IP allowlisting) on top of Team. Most small and boutique firms fit Team first: 2 to 150 seats, the same no-training default.

[ The short version ]

  • Enterprise pricing, as the pricing page states it: $20 per seat per month plus usage billed at API rates, billed annually, self-serve or through sales.
  • The pricing page publishes no Enterprise seat minimum; the help center does: 20 seats self-serve, 50 sales-assisted, read July 30, 2026. Team runs 2 to 150.
  • Model training is listed as none by default for Team and both Enterprise tiers, so the no-training default is not the reason to upgrade.
  • The real Enterprise reasons are the controls: SCIM, audit logs, custom data retention, network-level access control, IP allowlisting, and a HIPAA-ready offering.
  • One more difference: the pricing page lists a 500k context window on the default model for Enterprise, against 200k on Team.

A law firm asking about Claude Enterprise usually wants plainer answers than a procurement guide gives: the actual price structure, what Enterprise adds that Team does not have, and whether a 5-lawyer or a 15-lawyer firm needs it at all. The answers below are quoted from Anthropic’s own pages and dated.

The two plans, side by side

The training row is the same on both plans: Anthropic lists model training as none by default across Team and both Enterprise tiers, so nobody needs Enterprise to get the no-training default. What Enterprise sells is control and capacity.

Most firms fit Team first

Team is stated as for teams of 2 to 150, which covers the seat count of nearly any small or boutique firm. The seats are $20 per month billed annually ($25 monthly), premium seats at $100 ($125 monthly) add more usage, and the plan already includes Claude Code and Claude Cowork, single sign-on with domain capture, role-based permissioning, central billing with spend controls, admin controls for connectors, and the no-training default.

The arithmetic at small-firm sizes, from those listed prices: a 5-lawyer firm on standard seats is $100 a month billed annually; a 15-lawyer firm is fifteen standard seats at the listed $20 each; where a partner needs more usage, a premium seat runs $100 in place of a $20 seat. Team usage rides inside the seat price under the plan’s limits (standard seats run 1.25x Pro usage per session and premium seats 6.25x, per the Team help article), which keeps the monthly number predictable. Enterprise bills usage separately.

What Enterprise is actually for

Enterprise keeps the $20 seat price and bills usage separately, annually: the help center states the seat fee covers access only, with usage across Claude, Claude Code and Cowork billed at standard API rates, and admin spend limits at organization and user level. The feature list matches what security reviews ask for: SCIM, audit logs, a compliance API, custom data retention controls, network-level access control, IP allowlisting, and a HIPAA-ready offering. The pricing page also lists one capacity difference that matters for legal work: a 500k context window on the default model, against 200k on Team.

[ Checklist ]

Signs your firm actually needs Enterprise

  • Identity management is a requirement, not a preference. Your IT or a client's outside counsel guidelines require SCIM provisioning and fine-grained role-based permissioning. Single sign-on and standard role-based permissioning are already on Team.

  • Someone must be able to audit usage. Audit logs and the compliance API are Enterprise features. If your malpractice carrier or a client demands usage records, this is the tier that produces them.

  • Retention must match a written policy. Custom data retention controls are listed under Enterprise. A firm with a records schedule that vendors must follow needs that control.

  • Network controls are mandated. Network-level access control and IP allowlisting are listed under Enterprise's additions.

  • Health matters touch the work. The HIPAA-ready offering is named under Enterprise, and the help center states eligible organizations can enable it and accept a BAA from organization settings.

If none of those describe your firm: stay on Team, and revisit when a client’s security questionnaire, not a feature list, forces the question.

Two facts worth stating plainly

  • The pricing page publishes no Enterprise seat minimum, and the Enterprise page shows no price. The numbers live in Anthropic’s help center, read July 30, 2026: a 20-seat minimum self-serve, 50 sales-assisted. For a 5-lawyer or a 15-lawyer firm, the seat floor alone settles the question in Team’s favor.
  • Enterprise can be started self-serve per the pricing page’s own FAQ, with usage credits purchased up front; the sales-assisted path bills usage monthly in arrears.

05 / FAQ

The questions, answered

What does Claude Enterprise cost for a law firm?

Anthropic's pricing page states it directly: $20 per seat per month plus usage billed at API rates, so cost scales with the models and tasks your team runs, billed annually. The help center adds that the seat fee covers access only. There is no published flat total, and any budget number depends on usage.

Is there a minimum number of seats for Enterprise?

The pricing page publishes no seat minimum, but Anthropic's help center does, read July 30, 2026: Enterprise requires 20 seats self-serve and 50 through sales. Team is stated as for teams of 2 to 150. For nearly every small or boutique firm, the seat floor alone answers the plan question.

Does Claude Enterprise train on our client data?

The pricing page lists model training as none by default for Team and both Enterprise tiers, and Anthropic's privacy center states it does not use inputs or outputs from its commercial products to train models by default. The upgrade decision is about controls and scale, not about the training default.

Is the Team plan enough for a small or boutique firm?

Usually yes. Team is stated as for teams of 2 to 150, carries the no-training default, includes Claude Code and Claude Cowork, single sign-on, central billing and admin controls for connectors, and mixes standard with premium seats. A 5-lawyer firm on standard seats pays $100 a month billed annually, by the page's own numbers.

What is the 500k context window worth in practice?

It is the difference in how much the model can read at once: the pricing page lists 500k on the default model for Enterprise against 200k elsewhere, two and a half times the reading per pass. For document-heavy matters, that means fewer split-ups of one large file set, with the same review duties on the output.

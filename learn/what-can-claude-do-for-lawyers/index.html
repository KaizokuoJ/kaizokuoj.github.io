What can Claude do for lawyers?

By Jordy Backes Published 2026-09-04 Facts checked 2026-08-30

Answer

Five things a small firm can check: review a contract against the firm's positions and redline it in Word, draft a demand letter from the whole matter file with every citation checked, read a matter of about fifty documents and hand back the numbers to the cent, draft client updates, and take work assigned by email. All review-ready, never send-ready.

[ The short version ]

  • The useful work is document work: a contract redlined in Word, a demand letter as a Word file, a matter read back with every amount traced to its file.
  • Each task in the recordings started from the firm's own files, the contract, the invoices, the written positions, and the quality followed from what it read.
  • It stops at judgment calls: the tone of a letter, a clause no position covers, a number a lawyer must set. Those come back as flags and placeholders.
  • It sends nothing outside the firm. In every recording the draft waited for a lawyer.
  • The first task runs on a fictional matter with no client data in it, and the prompt on this page is that task.

Underneath this question sits a plainer one: what would it do on a Monday. This page lists the firm work Claude did in three builds recorded start to finish and published on this site, every one on a synthetic matter: fake firm, fake clients, real workflows. The counts are the ones that appeared on screen, and each section links to the page that shows it.

The work, task by task

Five tasks, three recordings. The right column is what came back; the sections below link to the page that shows each one.

Contract review: a redline in Word

The recorded review started from two files in one folder, the NDA and eight positions the firm had written in plain English, and one typed command. About 8 minutes later two files came back: a ranked risk memo, and the contract itself with native tracked changes, every edit traced to the position that required it. Where no position covered a clause, it made no edit and flagged the clause instead. The difference from a chat answer is that the edits sit in the document as tracked changes, each with the position behind it. The whole procedure, the verification list and a prompt that needs no setup are on Can AI review a contract?

Drafting from the matter file: the demand letter

A demand letter is a claim about facts, and the facts live in documents. The recorded build handed Claude 51 documents and four typed commands. It researched the law, built the position, attacked its own position the way opposing counsel would, then drafted.

Underneath those four commands, 155 agents worked, more than fifty of them doing nothing but checking citations. Of the authorities they checked, 54 were cleared and 36 were flagged and dropped before the draft existed, including a statute repealed years earlier. The lawyer typed the tone and the deadline. What it takes to get that letter, and what to verify before it leaves, is on Can AI write a demand letter?

Reading a matter and keeping the numbers straight

The least dramatic task in the recordings may be the most useful one. Handed a folder of about fifty documents, Claude read every one, filed the matter in five sections, and read the balance back to the cent: $42,410.00 in principal, $1,873.96 in finance charges, $44,283.96 in total as of May 31. Then it listed three soft spots before any drafting started, one of them an invoice the counterparty’s records carried ninety dollars low, each one a lawyer’s call.

That read-back is the first task worth trying, and it runs on a fictional matter with no client data in it.

  1. 01

    Build a fictional matter as files

    A made-up client, a made-up dispute, and a handful of documents that look like real intake: a contract, a few invoices, an email thread. The setup page has a prompt that writes these into a folder for you. No client data touches the tool during the test.

    Result: A folder of fictional documents that reads like a real matter.

  2. 02

    Point the desktop app at that folder

    In the Claude desktop app, the Code tab works inside one folder on your computer. Choose the folder with the fictional matter in it. Everything Claude reads and writes stays there.

    Result: The folder name shows above the message box.

  3. 03

    Paste the read-back prompt and check the numbers

    The prompt below asks for the parties, every amount with the file it came from, and the soft spots where the documents disagree. Check the total against your own arithmetic. In the recorded build this step is where an invoice ninety dollars low in the counterparty's records got caught without anyone asking.

    Result: A matter summary with every amount traced to a file, and a list of soft spots for a lawyer to decide.

Client updates and work by email

In the third recording the work arrived by email. A lawyer forwarded a contract with one line, and the redline came back in the same thread about four minutes later. The demand letter arrived about half an hour after it was assigned by email, with an explanation memo naming the files behind each point and a client update ready to forward, its balance to the cent and bracketed placeholders where a lawyer confirms.

A one line reply asking for a five year confidentiality term produced the corrected redline about two minutes later. The build, screen by screen, is Hire a Claude AI Employee for Your Law Firm.

What it does not do

Honest limits, from the three recordings:

  • It sends nothing outside the firm. Every draft waited for a lawyer, and in the email build it could mail only the firm’s own addresses.
  • It knows the law it is given, and the law it verifies at the source. In the emailed demand letter every statute was checked at Delaware’s own code site before use, and one authority that did not cleanly hold up was left out. A citation from memory is unverified until a lawyer reads it.
  • It does not decide. The tone, the deadline, the clause no position covers, the number a lawyer must set: each came back as a question or a placeholder, and the recordings stop there.

Review-ready, never send-ready. A licensed attorney approves everything before it leaves the firm. The setup that makes the first task possible, from the account to the working folder, is its own page: How do I set up Claude for my law firm?

07 / FAQ

The questions, answered

What do lawyers use AI for most?

Per the 8am 2026 Legal Industry Report, lawyers use generative AI most for drafting correspondence and for general research, both at 58%. The recordings on this site go one step further, into the matter file: contract review against written positions, a demand letter from the whole matter, a matter read back with every amount traced to its document.

Can Claude write a demand letter?

Yes, a review-ready draft, and the quality is decided by what it reads. In the recorded build the letter was drafted from 51 documents after the case had been researched, built and attacked, and a fresh review caught a contradiction in the closing, so the corrected letter saved as a second version. The full walk, with the prompt, is on Can AI write a demand letter?

Can Claude review a contract?

Yes, as a redline in Word rather than comments in a chat, when it has the firm's positions to review against. In the build video the review covered 17 clauses, made 12 redlines, raised 2 red flags and left 2 items for a lawyer, in about 8 minutes. The procedure and the verification list are on Can AI review a contract?

Does Claude send emails or file anything?

Nothing leaves the firm in any build on this site. Every recording ends with a draft in front of a lawyer: a redline to accept or reject, a letter to sign, a client update to forward. In the email build it could mail only the firm's own addresses, and the correction round was a lawyer replying to that email.

Does it need a special app or a paid plan?

The recordings run in the Claude desktop app's Code tab, which Anthropic lists on the paid plans Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise; Chat runs on every plan including Free. None of this involves writing code, and a lawyer who can use the regular chat can run the matter read-back prompt inside a folder.

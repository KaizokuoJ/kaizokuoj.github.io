Does Claude train on client data?

By Jordy Backes Published 2026-08-03 Facts checked 2026-07-30

Answer

It depends on the plan class. On Team, Enterprise and the API, Anthropic states customer data is not used to train its models by default. On Free, Pro and Max, training permission is a setting the account holder controls. Client data adds a duty no setting covers: the firm controls the account and decides what goes in.

[ The short version ]

  • Plan class decides the default: commercial plans (Team, Enterprise, API) do not train on your content by default; personal plans (Free, Pro, Max) leave the choice to the account holder.
  • On a personal plan, the setting is called Help improve our AI models. Turn it off before any client-adjacent work, and note the date.
  • Training off is not the same as deleted: turning the setting off keeps the existing 30-day retention period, and content flagged for usage policy violations can be kept up to 2 years.
  • No setting answers the confidentiality duty by itself. The firm still decides which documents go in, and a licensed attorney still reviews what comes out.

A law firm’s version of the training question is narrower than the general one: does Claude train on my clients’ data. The answer has two layers, what Anthropic publishes about training, and what the firm must decide because the data belongs to clients. Both layers fit on this one page, quote by quote, checked on the date in the byline.

What Anthropic publishes, plan by plan

The plan class decides the default, and the split is clean. For its commercial products, Anthropic’s privacy center states: “By default, we will not use your inputs or outputs from our commercial products (e.g. Claude for Work, Anthropic API, Claude Gov, etc.) to train our models.” The pricing page carries the same split as a table row: model training is listed as none by default for Team and Enterprise, and as opt-out for Free, Pro and Max.

On the personal plans, the choice covers more than chat: Anthropic names “Claude Free, Pro, and Max plans, including when they use Claude Code from accounts associated with those plans.” So a solo lawyer running Claude Code under a Pro subscription is inside the same consumer training choice as the chat window.

Commercial plans put training off by default and under contract; personal plans put it behind a setting that belongs to whoever owns the account, which is usually not the firm.

Training off is not the same as deleted

The training setting governs training, not storage. With training on, Anthropic states chats can be retained in de-identified form for up to 5 years for training. With it off, the existing 30-day retention period applies. Deleting a conversation removes it from your history immediately and clears Anthropic’s storage within 30 days, and content flagged for usage policy violations can be kept up to 2 years.

For a firm this matters in two directions. None of it is zero: a firm that told a client “nothing leaves our systems” will not find that sentence in these terms, which is what the check below is for. And the windows are short, stated in writing, which is a real protection.

The check, done in one sitting

Four steps, in order, one sitting.

  1. 01

    Find out which plan class your account runs under

    A personal subscription (Free, Pro, Max) runs under Anthropic's consumer terms, and the training choice covers those plans including their Claude Code use. Team, Enterprise and the API run under commercial terms, where Anthropic states there is no training on your content by default.

    Result: You know whose terms apply before any document goes in.

  2. 02

    Turn off model training on any personal account the firm touches

    The direct route is claude.ai/settings/data-privacy-controls. Anthropic's help article walks the route for desktop and for mobile. New accounts pick a preference at signup, so open the page even if you never changed anything.

    In the app: Your name (settings menu) → Settings → Privacy → Help improve our AI models → off

    Result: Anthropic states new chats and coding sessions will not be used for future model training.

  3. 03

    Check the setting against what your engagement letters promise

    The published terms are one half; the firm's own promises are the other. If your engagement letters or your client's outside counsel guidelines say vendor tools never learn from client material, the account settings and plan class need to match that sentence.

    Result: The firm's AI setup and its written promises say the same thing.

  4. 04

    Write the allowed-documents list and date the check

    One page: which document types may go into Claude, which never do, and the date someone last verified the terms. Many firms start with fictional test matters only, and widen the list as the firm's AI policy settles.

    Result: A defensible record of what the firm decided, and when.

When a vendor sits between you and Claude

When a legal tool runs on Claude through the API, the no-training default covers the model layer, and the vendor’s own terms cover everything else: what the vendor stores, for how long, and what it does with derived data. If the answers do not come in writing, the firm has nothing to file. This block is ready to paste into an email.

The duty layer no setting reaches

The confidentiality duty does not ban cloud tools, and it does not end at a toggle either. The standard the American Bar Association applies to generative AI in Formal Opinion 512 is the familiar one: competence with the tools the firm uses, protection of client confidences, and supervision of nonlawyer assistance. The mechanical side sits with the firm: someone reads the terms that apply to the firm’s own plan, the firm decides which matters and document types are allowed in, and a licensed attorney reviews the output before anyone relies on it.

06 / FAQ

The questions, answered

Does Claude train on my data by default?

On Team, Enterprise and the API, no: Anthropic states it does not use your inputs or outputs from its commercial products to train models by default. On Free, Pro and Max, there is no single default: the account picks a preference at signup, and the setting can be changed any time.

If I turn training off, is my data deleted?

No. Turning training off keeps the existing 30-day retention period; it does not erase anything by itself. Deleting a conversation removes it from your history immediately and from Anthropic's storage within 30 days. Content flagged for policy violations can be retained up to 2 years.

Do Team and Enterprise plans train on our documents?

Anthropic states customer data from its commercial products is not used for training by default, and its pricing page lists model training as none by default for Team and both Enterprise tiers. The commitment sits in the plan terms.

What about legal tools built on Claude?

The vendor's own terms sit between you and Anthropic, so the API default only covers the model layer. Ask the vendor in writing whether your data trains any model, which Claude offering it runs on, and what its retention periods are. The question block on this page is ready to send.

Is turning the toggle off enough to make client data safe?

No single setting carries the confidentiality duty. The setting answers one question, training. The firm still chooses the plan class, decides which documents are allowed in at all, and keeps a licensed attorney reviewing everything that comes out. Many firms run their first tests on fictional matters only.

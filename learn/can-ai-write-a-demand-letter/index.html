Can AI write a demand letter?

By Jordy Backes Published 2026-08-05 Facts checked 2026-07-30

Answer

Yes, a review-ready draft; the quality is decided by what it reads. With the full matter file and a confirmed position, the draft comes back in Word for attorney review. In the build video behind this page, 155 agents worked on one letter drafted from 51 documents of a synthetic matter. A licensed attorney approves everything before it leaves.

[ The short version ]

  • AI drafts a demand letter well when it reads the matter file first: the contract, the invoices, the correspondence, not a two-line summary.
  • The strong workflow builds the case before the letter: research, position, then an attack pass playing opposing counsel, so the draft rests on what survived.
  • The judgment calls stay typed in by the lawyer: tone, response window, what happens when the deadline passes.
  • Every citation and figure gets verified against its source before the letter leaves; the checklist on this page is that procedure.
  • Review-ready, never send-ready: a licensed attorney approves the letter, the same way an associate's draft gets approved.

AI can produce demand-letter-shaped text, and so can a web form. A law firm’s question is narrower: can it produce a letter a lawyer would sign, with correct figures, verified authority, the firm’s own tone, and the actual matter file underneath. It can, and the numbers on this page come from one build video recorded start to finish, on a synthetic matter: fake firm, fake clients, real workflows.

What decides the answer: the matter file

A demand letter is a claim about facts, and the facts live in documents. Give AI the two-sentence version and it writes confident filler. Give it the signed contract, the invoices, the correspondence and the logs, and the draft starts citing the dates, amounts and contract sections in those files. In the recorded build the difference was visible on screen: 51 documents went in, and the matter summary, chronology and claims came out anchored to them, file by file.

AI writes a strong demand letter exactly when it is allowed to read what a paralegal would read.

The workflow that produced a review-ready letter

The recorded build ran as five moves, and the same five moves usually transfer to any firm.

  1. 01

    Assemble the matter folder before anything drafts

    Everything the client handed you, exactly as it is: the signed contract, the change orders or amendments, the invoices, the email thread, the photos or logs. The recorded build ran on 51 documents. A demand letter drafted from a two-line summary reads like one.

    Result: One folder that holds the facts the letter must rest on.

  2. 02

    Build the case first, not the letter

    The recorded workflow researches the law, builds the position from the documents, then attacks its own position the way opposing counsel would. In that run, 54 citations cleared verification and 36 were flagged and dropped, including a statute repealed years earlier whose old text still circulated.

    Result: A position the draft can rest on, with the weak claims already softened or cut.

  3. 03

    Type the judgment calls yourself

    Tone, the response window, what happens when the deadline passes: those are the lawyer's calls, and the workflow stops and asks rather than assuming them. In the recorded run the letter demanded payment within fourteen days, with interest stated at 1.5% per month.

    Result: A draft built on your terms, not on a template's defaults.

  4. 04

    Open the draft in Word and trace lines to sources

    The deliverable is a Word document with the demand, the facts and the legal basis, filed with the rest of the matter. Spot check any sentence back to the file it came from before review begins.

    Result: A review-ready draft whose claims you can follow to their sources.

  5. 05

    Run a fresh review, then the attorney signs

    The recorded build reviews in a new session with no memory of writing the letter: citations rechecked against official sources, quotes against documents, claims against evidence. Its verdict on the first draft was needs changes, with one high-severity contradiction found in the closing.

    Result: A letter that survived its own audit, approved by a licensed attorney before it leaves.

What to verify before it leaves

Each item below names where the recorded workflow handles it.

[ Checklist ]

Verify these before any demand letter leaves the firm

  • The amounts. Recompute the principal, the interest arithmetic, and the total demanded, from the source invoices rather than from the draft. Step 4 traces figures back to their files.

  • Names, dates and parties. Every name, date, address and party designation against the matter documents. A wrong extraction reads as carelessness to opposing counsel.

  • Every citation, against the official source. Any statute or case named in the letter gets opened and read, not recognized. Courts have sanctioned lawyers for filing AI-invented citations. Step 5 runs the fresh-session check.

  • Quotes, verbatim. Anything in quotation marks matches the underlying document character for character.

  • The closing paragraph against the letter's own terms. The recorded build's review caught the draft's closing quietly waiving the interest and fees the letter had just reserved. Read the last paragraph against the first. Step 5 is where that catch happened.

The duties behind that list are the familiar ones, the same rules the American Bar Association applies to generative AI in Formal Opinion 512: competence, confidentiality, and supervision of work that is not the lawyer’s own first draft. Nothing about AI changes who signs.

Where AI genuinely fails at this

Three honest limits:

  • It invents authority when it is allowed to cite from memory, and courts have sanctioned lawyers who filed those citations unchecked. Verification against official sources is the fix, and it is built into the workflow above.
  • It cannot judge what this opposing party, with this insurer, on this deadline, will actually respond to. That read stays with the lawyer.
  • It holds no view on whether sending a demand is the right move at all. The recorded build stops and asks instead of assuming.

06 / FAQ

The questions, answered

Can AI write a demand letter for free?

Free generators produce a generic letter from a form, which can fit a simple consumer dispute. Firm work fails there for a different reason: the letter is limited by the matter file behind it, and a form cannot read one. The recorded build linked on this page ships free, and it reads the whole file.

Can a law firm actually send an AI-drafted demand letter?

The letter a firm sends is the firm's letter, whoever drafted the first pass. It goes out after a licensed attorney has reviewed and approved it, exactly like an associate's draft. The professional duties in play, competence, confidentiality and supervision, are the same ones the ABA maps onto generative AI in Formal Opinion 512.

How long does it take?

In the recorded build, reading all 51 matter documents took about 12 minutes and the full case build about an hour. With the drafting questions, the fresh review pass and attorney sign-off on top, plan for an afternoon from raw matter folder to reviewed letter.

What does AI get wrong in demand letters?

The documented failure is invented authority: citations that look real and are not. The quieter failures are wrong figures, a missed date, and a draft that contradicts its own terms. Every one of those is catchable before the letter leaves, which is what the verification list on this page is for.

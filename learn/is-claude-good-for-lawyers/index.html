Is Claude AI good for lawyers?

By Jordy Backes Published 2026-09-04 Facts checked 2026-08-30

Answer

Good at document work that reads the firm's own files: in three build videos it redlined an NDA in Word in about 8 minutes, drafted a demand letter from 51 documents, and read a fifty document matter back to the cent. Not good as an unsupervised authority: it can cite what does not exist, so a licensed attorney reviews everything.

[ The short version ]

  • Claude is good for a law firm when it reads the same files a paralegal would, and a lawyer reads its output.
  • In the build videos the output was a document: an NDA redlined in Word with tracked changes, a demand letter as a Word file, a matter read back to the cent.
  • Where it could not verify something it stopped: no edit on an unverified clause, a bracketed placeholder instead of an invented interest figure, one authority left out of a letter.
  • It is not a legal database. A citation it produces from memory is unverified until a lawyer reads it at the source.
  • The benchmark number is low because the grading is strict: Harvey's all-pass standard scores Fable 5 at 13.3%, and a task counts only when finished end to end.

The question underneath this one is whether a small firm should bother. This page answers it from three builds recorded start to finish and published on this site, each on a synthetic matter: fake firm, fake clients, real workflows. The counts below are the ones that appeared on screen.

The short verdict

Claude is good for a law firm under two conditions, and both are the firm’s to meet. It reads the firm’s own files: the contract, the invoices, the positions the firm wrote down, instead of a two-line summary. And a lawyer reads what it wrote before anyone relies on it.

Under those two conditions the recorded work was a document a lawyer could sign after review. Without them, it is a chat answer nobody has checked. The cost is the setup: the positions file and the folder have to exist before the first run, and only the firm can write the positions. It makes up for that in traceability, because every edit it made in the recordings pointed back at a line the firm wrote.

What it did well, task by task

Four tasks, three recordings, all on synthetic matters. The right-hand column is the part that stays the lawyer’s.

Two of those rows carry the detail. The demand letter build put 155 agents on one letter, more than fifty of them doing nothing but checking citations against sources, and 54 authorities were cleared while 36 were flagged and dropped before the draft existed, including a statute repealed years earlier whose old text still circulated.

In the matter read-back, the discrepancy it flagged unprompted was ninety dollars: the counterparty’s records carried one invoice at $13,560 against the $13,650.00 the documents supported. That is the kind of catch a careful paralegal makes.

Where it stopped and asked

The strongest evidence on the three recordings is the refusals, and they are checkable on screen.

  • In the NDA review, two clauses pointed at an exhibit and a security standard that were never provided. Neither clause received an edit; both were listed in the memo with what to request from the other side.
  • In the model comparison on the same NDA, one model accepted a venue objection waiver as satisfied. The other made no edit, because no written position covered the waiver, and flagged it for attorney judgment.
  • In the emailed demand letter, forward interest through the payment date needed a lawyer’s call, so the draft carried a bracketed placeholder where the number would go instead of a guess.

In the recordings, when it could not verify a clause it made no edit and flagged the clause for a lawyer instead of guessing. A firm that understands that behavior can put real work behind it.

Where it is not good

Honest limits, from the recordings and from the public record:

  • Authority from memory. Left to cite on its own, a model can produce a case that does not exist, and courts have sanctioned lawyers who filed those citations unchecked. A Stanford RegLab study of leading AI legal research tools found them hallucinating between 17% and 33% of the time. The recorded builds treat every citation as unverified until it is read at the official source, and the prompt below does the same.
  • Judgment about the deal. It cannot tell you whether this counterparty will accept the pushback, whether the flagged clause is worth the fight, or whether sending the letter is the right move at all. Each recording stops at those points and asks.
  • Anything unsupervised. It is not a legal database, it does not know the firm’s client, and it holds no license. Review-ready, never send-ready: a licensed attorney approves everything before it leaves the firm.

The benchmark, read honestly

Harvey publishes a Legal Agent Benchmark graded on an all-pass standard: the model completes an entire legal task end to end, or the task counts as a fail. On that grading Fable 5 scores 13.3% and the model before it, Opus 4.8, scores 10.4%. The numbers look low because the grading is strict, and that strictness is what makes them worth quoting. On this site’s own NDA, both models caught both red flags and both items needing input; the three differences between them are on screen on the build page.

Crosby, an AI native law firm, reported from blind review at launch that Fable 5’s redlines “matched or beat our current model every time.” The recording on this site lines up with that phrasing: matched or beat, with no dramatic gap.

Test it yourself, on no client data

A verdict from someone else’s recordings is a reason to test, and no more. Four steps, no client data.

  1. 01

    Build a fictional matter

    A made-up client, a made-up dispute, and two or three files that look like your real intake. The prompts on the pages Is Claude safe for lawyers? and How do I set up Claude for my law firm? have Claude write this for you. No client data touches the tool during the test.

    Result: A realistic test file that contains no client information.

  2. 02

    Run one real piece of firm work on it

    A contract review makes the best first test, because a redline is easy to judge against your own. Give it the contract and three or four of the firm's positions written in plain English, and ask for the redline. The full procedure, with the prompt, is on Can AI review a contract?

    Result: A document to judge, in the form the firm already uses.

  3. 03

    Review the draft in a fresh session

    Open a new chat that has never seen the matter, paste the fresh-review prompt below with the draft and the source documents, and read the report. The recorded demand letter build did exactly this, and the fresh reviewer caught the first draft's closing paragraph quietly waiving the interest and fees the letter had just reserved.

    Result: A source-by-source check, with every citation left marked unverified for a lawyer to open.

  4. 04

    Decide, on the evidence

    Put the AI draft beside the one a junior at the firm would have written, count what you had to fix, and look at the time the run took. The verdict on this page came from reading three recordings; yours should come from your own.

    Result: A verdict for your firm, from your own matter and your own reviewer.

The fresh-review step is the easiest one to skip, and it is the one that caught the contradiction in the recorded demand letter. Paste this into a new chat that has never seen the matter.

07 / FAQ

The questions, answered

Is Claude better than ChatGPT for lawyers?

This site has not run the two on the same firm task side by side, so it makes no claim either way. The honest test is the same fictional matter through both, the same positions, and a lawyer judging the two redlines. What the recordings do show is how Claude behaves when it cannot verify something: it stops and flags.

Can Claude do legal research?

It can read the law you give it, and in one recorded build it checked every statute in a demand letter at the state's own code site before use. It is not a legal database. A citation it produces from memory is unverified until a lawyer opens it at the source, and a fresh-review prompt run in a new chat marks every citation that way.

Is Claude safe for client documents?

Safety rests on three firm choices: the plan, the privacy settings, and attorney review on every output. On the Team plan, the Enterprise plan and the API, Anthropic states customer data is not used for training by default; on personal plans it is a toggle. The full picture, quote by quote, is on Is Claude safe for lawyers?

Which Claude plan do lawyers need?

Chat runs on every plan including Free. The builds on this site run in the desktop app's Code tab, which Anthropic lists on the paid plans Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise. A firm that wants to control the settings picks Team or Enterprise. Prices, dated, are on How much does Claude cost for lawyers?

Can Claude replace a paralegal or an associate?

No, and the recorded builds show why. It drafts, organizes and checks, and at every judgment call it stops: which tone, which deadline, whether a flagged clause is worth the fight, what number goes into a bracketed placeholder. Those decisions, the client contact and the signature stay with people at the firm.
